## ADS1115 ALERT Safety Interface

The ADS1115 ALERT output shall not directly energize any relay coil.

A single-channel 5V opto-isolated relay module with onboard transistor
driver and flyback protection shall be used as the interface between
the ADS1115 ALERT output and the contactor authorization circuit.

This relay module replaces the previously proposed automotive relay,
eliminating unnecessary hardware while protecting the ADS1115 from
relay-coil current.

The relay module shall interrupt the contactor authorization path upon
an ALERT condition, placing the system into its safe shutdown state.

=========================================================
            CONTACTOR INTERLOCK / BREAK-BEFORE-MAKE
=========================================================
Purpose:
Guarantees The LEV 100 Banks 12v Contactor Releases Before
The 16v EV200 Main Boost Path Contactor Is Allowed To Energize.
Must Work For Both Transition To And Detransition From 16v Boost Function

Theory Of Operation:
1 SPDT 12v In Its Relaxed (Unpowered) State Turns The 12v Lev100 Banks Control Relay To Authorized,
Allowing The Timer Switch Signals To Reach The Controls.
The Open Pole Of Same Double Throw Relay Assures 16v EV200 Contactor Is NOT Authorized. 
This Is Probably Sufficient As A Safety Interlock To Insure Either Contactor Bank Can Never Be On At The Same Time,
However,
There Is Concern For The Amount Of Amps Being Pushed At The Moment Of Commanded Contactor 
Switchover Could Pose A Risk (Even If Small) Of The 2 Voltages Running Into Each Other.
Which Could Weld The Contacts Together Microseconds After The Commanded Switchover.
So It Should Be Simple Enough To Introduce A Controlled Delay Long Enough To Guarantee 
12v Contactor Has Released Before The 16v Contactor Can Engage.
The Resultant Burn Down Of A 5p5s Pack From Such An Unlikely But Not Zero Chance Event Could Be Quite Energetic
To Say The Least, So Extra Precautions Are Being Employed By Stretching Out
The Sequence Using 2 Relays To Double The Time Alotted For Contactor Disconnect Before Connect 
" Break For Sure Before Make " And Still Accomplish The Switchover In No Less >50ms No More Than 100 milliseconds
And Works For Both Transition To And Detransition From 16v Boost Function.

Function:
12V OFF
    ↓
Delay By Dual Consecutive Relay, NOT DBL Throw, 
An Actual 2nd Relay For Pole/Pivot Transit Travel Time x2 
Best Guess This Will Give Us >50ms Delay, 
This Provides The Same Delay In Both Directions.
Hopefully Preventing Pack Shorting By Contact Overlap 
Resulting In Welded Contactors From An Accidental Make-Before-Break. 
Unlikely, But Not Zero Chance So... Extra Precautions Are Being Employed.
    ↓
16V ON

The 12V Path Logic: 
Run The Main Control Power Into Relay 2, Pin 30. 
Solder A Wire From Its Normally Closed Contact (Pin 87a) Over To Relay 1, Pin 30. 
Finally, Run Relay 1'S Normally Open Contact (Pin 87) Out To The LEV100 Contactor Coils.

The 16V Path Logic: 
Mirror The Exact Same Step On The Other Side. Run Main Control Power Into Relay 1, Pin 30. 
Solder A Wire From Its Normally Closed Contact (Pin 87a) Over To Relay 2, Pin 30. 
Then, Run Relay 2'S Normally Open Contact (Pin 87) Out To The Big EV200 Contactor Coils.

            Processor Commanded Lockout:
=========================================================
By Using The ADS1115 As The Ground Switch, The Loop Is Simple:
The Positive Side: You Run 12V Straight To The Positive Leg Of The Relay Coil.
The Negative Side: You Run A Wire From The Negative Leg Of That Relay Coil Straight To The ADS1115 ALRT Pin.
The Operation:
System Fault: The Display Commands The ADS1115 To Close Its Internal Switch (Go Ground) Upon An Error Or Shutdown Command.
The ALRT Pin Connects Directly To Ground, The 12V Relay Activates, 
In Order For The Pack To Work Without A Micro-controller Functioning, We Wire 
The SPDT Relay As Authorized In Its Relaxed Position
Allowing POS+ Power to Pass, In That Mode We Therefore Depend On Other Control Measures To Insure Pack Safety.
During A NO-Controller Scenario 
Only Adding Power To The Relay POS+ Leg By Command Will Break The Relaxed Pole And 
Interrupt Operation And The System Loses The Authority To Run By Breaking Path Of Relay Coil POS+
While Other Measures Control The Path Of Relay Coil Neg-.

Run ALRT Wire To The Negative Leg Of The Existing 12V Auth Interupt Relay Coil And You Are Done.

            PROTECTION
=========================================================
The P6KE18A Is A Transient Voltage Suppressor (TVS) Diode. 
Under Normal Conditions, It Sits Completely Dormant And Invisible. 
However, If A Massive Voltage Spike 
(Like A Starter Motor Inductive Kickback Or A Surface Charge Surge) 
Hits The System, It Snaps Alive Instantly To Clamp And Shunt That Energy Safely 
To Ground Before It Can Travel Down The Small Signal Wires
Because The 5S Full Pack Max Voltage Is 18.25V, 
We Have Selected An 18V TVS To Act As A Threshold Guard.
On The Divider Board Right Before The Signal Hits The Resistors. 
They Belong Directly Across Each Raw Incoming Battery Tap Line 
Stripe To Positive With All Of Their Ends At System Ground

The Power Reference: 
The Shifter Needs To Know What Both "High" And "Low" Mean. 
Connect 3.3V+ To The LV Pin, And Connect The Main 5V+ Rail To The HV Pin.

Common Ground: Tie The GND Pins On Both Sides Of The Shifter To Pack Ground/Negative.

Board 1: (4 BMS Taps Starting At Pack Positive +):
We Got Lucky Here As Apparently The 1115 Board Will Auto Address Itself If The 
Address Hole Is Left Empty Which Is What Happened, So No Harm No Foul, 
It Should Adress Itself To Address 0x48

Board 2: (Remaining BMS Tap Cell 5 + Current Clamp): 
Solder A Tiny Jumper Wire From The ADDR Pin Straight To The 5v+ VDD Pin On This Board. 
This Names It Address 0x49

The Data Path: SDA And SCL From The Twin ADS1115 Boards Are Tied Together Then They Go Into HV1 And HV2. 
They Exit Cleanly Through LV1 And LV2 Straight To The Elecrow Display

The Power References: HV Gets The Clean 5V+ Rail (From The Buck Converter), 
And LV Gets The Reference 3.3V+ From Magic 3.3V+ Maker

The Ground Plane: Both Grounds On The Shifter Tie Together Right Into The Central System Ground

The Spares: Channels 3 And 4 (LV3, LV4, HV3, HV4) Stay Completely Empty And Unpopulated, 
Serving As Perfect Backups If A Channel Ever Gets Cooked 

Direct Channel Passthrough: 
Align The Data Lines Perfectly. 
If Clock (SCL) Goes Into LV1, 
It Must Exit Through HV1 To Go To The ADS1115 Boards. 
If Data (SDA) Goes Into LV2, It Must Exit Through HV2.
Dont Forget To Set The Address Of The Boards Before You Stack Them.

The 3.3V Node: 
The middle junction between the 10kΩ and 20kΩ resistors is the central distribution hub. 
It powers the Level Shifter's LV pin, feeds the 5 temperature probe red wires, 
and provides the baseline power entering the top of The 4.7kΩ pull-up resistor.

The Data Node: 
The bottom leg of that 4.7kΩ pull-up resistor must tie into the yellow data lines of The 
temperature probesOutput Header Pin 8 and Pin 7 (TX) on The 12-pin breakout board simultaneously.

The Ground Node: 
The bottom leg of the 20kΩ resistor, and the screen ground tracks (Pins 1 & 2), 
and the 5 black temperature sensor wires all meet right back at System Ground


MASTER 3.3V MODULE NOTES
Step 1 Create The Core Junction:
Take One 10k Resistor And One 20k Resistor. 
Twist And Solder One Leg Of Each Resistor Together In Mid Air. 
This Shared Connection Point Is The Official 3.3V Node.

Step 2 Attach The 10k Resistor, Solder It Directly Onto Header Pin 8 Which Is The 5V+ Main Power Input. 
Take The Leg Of The 20k Resistor And Solder It Straight To The System Ground 
Which Can Be Header Pin 7 Or Any Sys GND-. You Now Have A Rigid V Shape Sticking Up From The Header.

Step 3 Connect The Targets To The 3.3V Node Go Straight To That Floating Middle 3.3V Node You Created In Step 1. 
Solder The 4.7k Temperature Sensor Master Pull Up Resistor Directly To That Node. 
Solder All 5 Of The Temperature Sensor Red VCC Wires Directly To That 3.3V Node. 
Solder A Single Wire Directly To That Node And Run It Straight Over To The LV Pin On The Logic Level Shifter.

Step 1 Locate The Central 3.33V Node Which Is The Middle Junction Where The Top 10k Ohm Resistor 
Meets The Bottom Two Chained 10k Ohm Resistors.

Step 2 Take The 4.7k Ohm Temperature Master Pull Up Resistor Which Has Yellow Violet Red Gold Bands. 
Solder One Leg Of This Resistor Directly Onto That Central 3.33V Node.

Step 3 Take All 5 Of The Red VCC Wires Coming From The Temperature Sensor Bundle. 
Solder Them Directly Onto That Same Central 3.33V Node So They Get Stable Low Voltage Power.

Step 4 Take The Remaining Loose Leg Of The 4.7k Ohm Resistor And Solder It Directly To 
Output Side Header Pin 8. This Is The Temperature Data Line.

Step 5 Take All 5 Of The Yellow Data Wires From The Temperature Sensor Bundle. 
Solder Them Directly To That Same Output Side Header Pin 8 Sharing The Pad With The 4.7k Ohm Resistor Leg.
