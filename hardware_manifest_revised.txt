=========================================================
            CONTACTOR INTERLOCK / BREAK-BEFORE-MAKE
=========================================================
Purpose:
Guarantees The LEV 100 Banks 12v Contactor Releases Before
The 16v EV200 Main Boost Path Contactor Is Allowed To Energize.
Must Work For Both Transition To And Detransition From 16v Boost Function

Theory Of Operation:
1 SPDT 12v In Its Relaxed (Unpowered) State Turns The 12v Lev100 Banks Control Relay To Authorized,
Allowing The Timer Switch Signals To Reach The Controls.
The Open Pole Of Same Double Throw Relay Assures 16v EV200 Contactor Is NOT Authorized. 
This Is Probably Sufficient As A Safety Interlock To Insure Either Contactor Bank Can Never Be On At The Same Time,
However,
There Is Concern For The Amount Of Amps Being Pushed At The Moment Of Commanded Contactor 
Switchover Could Pose A Risk (Even If Small) Of The 2 Voltages Running Into Each Other.
Which Could Weld The Contacts Together Microseconds After The Commanded Switchover.
So It Should Be Simple Enough To Introduce A Controlled Delay Long Enough To Guarantee 
12v Contactor Has Released Before The 16v Contactor Can Engage.
The Resultant Burn Down Of A 5p5s Pack From Such An Unlikely (But Not Zero Chance) Event Could Be Quite Energetic
To Say The Least, So Extra Precautions Are Being Employed By Stretching Out
The Sequence Using 2 Relays To Double The Time Alotted For Contactor Disconnect Before Connect 
" Break For Sure Before Make " And Still Accomplish The Switchover In No Less Than <50ms No More Than >100 milliseconds
And Works For Both Transition To And Detransition From 16v Boost Function.

Function:
12V OFF
    ↓
Delay By Dual Consecutive Relay, NOT DBL Throw, 
An Actual 2nd Relay For Pole/Pivot Transit Travel Time x2 
Best Guess This Will Give Us >50ms Delay, 
This Provides The Same Delay In Both Directions.
Hopefully Preventing Pack Shorting By Contact Overlap 
Resulting In Welded Contactors From An Accidental Make-Before-Break. 
Unlikely, But Not Zero Chance So... Extra Precautions Are Being Employed.
    ↓
16V ON

Location:
Service Hatch

The 12V Path Logic: 
Run The Main Control Power Into Relay 2, Pin 30. 
Run A Wire From Its Normally Closed Contact (Pin 87a) Over To Relay 1, Pin 30. 
Finally, Run Relay 1'S Normally Open Contact (Pin 87) Out To The LEV100 Contactor Coils.

The 16V Path Logic: 
Mirror The Exact Same Step On The Other Side. Run Main Control Power Into Relay 1, Pin 30. 
Run A Wire From Its Normally Closed Contact (Pin 87a) Over To Relay 2, Pin 30. 
Then, Run Relay 2'S Normally Open Contact (Pin 87) Out To The Big EV200 Contactor Coils.

            Processor Commanded Lockout:
=========================================================
The ADS1115 ALERT Output Does NOT Directly Energize A Relay Coil.

A Single-Channel 5V Opto-Isolated Relay Module Is Used As The Interface:
AITRIP 5V One Channel Relay Module
Opto Isolation / High-Low Level Trigger
ASIN: B096M77HCJ

The Module Contains Its Own Relay Driver And Flyback Protection,
So The ADS1115 ALERT Signal Does Not Carry Relay-Coil Current.

The Relay Contacts Are Wired Into The Positive Contactor Authorization Path.
The Authorized State Uses The Relaxed (Non-Powered) Relay Contact Arrangement That Allows The Pack
To Operate Without The Processor Actively Holding A Relay Coil Energized.

Upon A Processor / ALERT Shutdown Command, The Opto Relay Module Is Activated And Interrupts
The Positive Authorization Path And De-Energizes Both Contactor Banks.

This Module Replaces The Previously Proposed Automotive Gatekeeper Relay.

=========================================================
            PROTECTION
=========================================================
The P6KE18A Is A Transient Voltage Suppressor (TVS) Diode. 
Under Normal Conditions, It Sits Completely Dormant And Invisible. 
However, If A Massive Voltage Spike 
(Like A Starter Motor Inductive Kickback Or A Surface Charge Surge) 
Hits The System, It Snaps Alive Instantly To Clamp And Shunt That Energy Safely 
To Ground Before It Can Travel Down The Small Signal Wires
Because The 5S Full Pack Max Voltage Is 18.25V, 
We Have Selected An 18V TVS To Act As A Threshold Guard.
On The Divider Board Right Before The Signal Hits The Resistors. 
They Belong Directly Across Each Raw Incoming Protected Voltage Sense Line:
The Five Cumulative Battery Taps Plus Final Output Positive And Final Output Negative.
Stripe To Positive With All Of Their Ends At System Ground.

- After engine start, detect current returning to SNAPACK and track recovered charge.
- Display recovery progress and provide a clear indication when the energy used during the crank has been recovered.
- Cells 1–4 / normal 12V section receive charge directly from the vehicle.
- If the 12V section has recovered but Cell Group 5 / boost cell remains low, 
a future small internally controlled isolated LiFePO4 charger may transfer energy from the 12V section to Cell 5.
- This could provide a simple field-charging method: connect SNAPACK to a running vehicle and allow 
SNAPACK to manage its own recovery without specialized shop charging equipment.
- Specialized direct BMS/cell charging remains available for full service/balancing via 6s BMS leads at service hatch

AUTOMATIC DISCONNECT

During vehicle charging/recovery, continuously monitor:
- Charge current and direction
- Individual cell voltages
- Pack voltage
- Temperature sensors
- Recovered charge

When the desired recovery level is reached, or if a configured voltage/current/temperature limit is reached, SNAPACK may remove the authoritative positive relay authorization and open the main contactors, automatically disconnecting itself electrically from the vehicle.

Normal completion and fault-triggered shutdown should be separately identified and logged/displayed.

12-MINUTE HARD LIMIT

The existing 12-minute hardware timer remains authoritative and should not be bypassed for charging.

Software may terminate recovery earlier, but may not extend operation beyond the timer.

The 12-minute window already provides substantial potential recovery from a normally operating vehicle 
charging system and preserves the original hardware-enforced maximum connection time.

CELL 5 MAINTENANCE / TOP-OFF MODE

The proposed isolated Cell-5 LiFePO4 charger does not need to operate only during post-crank vehicle recovery.

Because Cell 5 top-off current will likely be relatively low (~1 A), Cell 5 may recover incrementally over multiple SNAPACK operating sessions.

A user may deliberately activate a normal 12V timer cycle while no vehicle is connected specifically to allow the 12V Cells 1–4 section to top off Cell 5.

Possible logic:

- Normal 12-minute SNAPACK timer is activated.
- No cranking or external vehicle-charging event is occurring.
- Firmware checks Cells 1–4 and Cell 5.
- If the 12V section has adequate available charge and Cell 5 is below its defined target, enable the isolated Cell-5 charger.
- Display indicates BOOST CELL TOP-OFF / Cell 5 voltage / charging status.
- Charger terminates normally when Cell 5 reaches its proper charge condition.
- Existing 12-minute hardware timer remains the absolute maximum session duration.
- Additional 12-minute maintenance sessions may be initiated later if Cell 5 still requires charging.

This allows Cell 5 maintenance while SNAPACK is being transported between jobs and reduces the importance of completing Cell-5 recovery during a single vehicle-charging session.

PROPOSED FEATURE — FIVE-CHANNEL SERVICE CHARGER

Possible future replacement for the present manual five-power-supply charging procedure.

Concept:
- Provide one external DC service-power connection.
- Internally split that source among five independent isolated DC/DC converters.
- Each isolated converter powers one dedicated 1S LiFePO4 charger such as the SK3624 3.6V / 2.4A module.
- Each charger independently charges one 1P5S cell group:

  Charger 1 → B0-B1
  Charger 2 → B1-B2
  Charger 3 → B2-B3
  Charger 4 → B3-B4
  Charger 5 → B4-B5

- The five isolated converter INPUTS may share the same external power source.
- Their OUTPUTS remain isolated so each charger can float at its respective position in the series pack.
- Individual chargers may terminate as their respective cell groups become full while the remaining groups continue charging.
- Approximate maximum combined cell-charging power at 2.4A/channel is ~43W, before conversion losses.

PACKAGING OPTIONS:

1. INTERNAL:
   Five isolated converters + five charger modules installed inside SNAPACK.
   User connects only one ordinary external service-power supply.

2. EXTERNAL:
   If enclosure space becomes insufficient, place the complete five-channel isolated charger system in a dedicated external service-charger box.
   Connect that charger to SNAPACK through the existing B0-B5 service/BMS connection.

This is separate from the proposed automatic onboard Cell-5 recovery charger used during post-crank/maintenance operation.

PROPOSED — AUTOMATIC CELL-5 CHARGE / RECOVERY HARDWARE

PURPOSE

Provide an onboard method of charging Cell Group 5 (B4-B5) from the
normal 12V section without requiring the normal 12V and Cell-5 grounds
to be common.

This supports:
- Post-crank Cell-5 recovery
- Intentional Cell-5 maintenance/top-off sessions
- Opportunistic Cell-5 charging whenever operating conditions permit


PROPOSED POWER PATH

12V section / available SNAPACK power
        |
        v
Cincon EC5DAW-24S05N isolated DC/DC
        |
        |  Isolated regulated 5V / 2A / 10W
        v
SK3624 1S LiFePO4 charger
        |
        +---- BAT+ -> B5
        |
        +---- BAT- -> B4

Cincon EC5DAW-24S05N:
- Input: 9-36VDC
- Output: regulated 5V / 2A
- Power: 10W
- Galvanically isolated
- May therefore be powered directly from the 12V section or from the
  existing 24V rail.

SK3624 charger:
- 1S LiFePO4
- 4.5-5.5V input
- 3.6V charge cutoff
- Up to approximately 2.4A charging
- Internal CC/CV charging and termination
- Approximately 200mA termination threshold
- Automatic recharge below approximately 3.5V

The isolated converter allows the complete SK3624 charger circuit to
float at B4 potential.

CELL-5 CHARGER CONTROL

ADS1115 Board 2 already monitors B5:

Investigate using Board 2 ALERT as the authoritative Cell-5 charger enable/disable signal.

ADS1115 ALERT is open-drain:
- Asserted = actively pulls LOW
- Inactive = releases line / high impedance
- External pull-up establishes HIGH state when released

Proposed control:

Board 2 ALERT
      |
   pull-up
      |
      v
Low-level-trigger opto-isolated relay/control stage
      |
      v
Cell-5 charger power authorization

Desired logic:
- ALERT released / HIGH = CHARGER OFF
- ALERT asserted / LOW = CHARGER ON

Therefore loss of ADS1115 power/configuration or an open ALERT signal
naturally results in charger OFF.

The ESP32 does not directly drive ALERT. It commands Board 2 over the
existing SDA/SCL I2C bus and can rapidly rewrite the ADS1115 comparator
configuration/threshold registers.

Board 2 may continue scanning its four analog channels normally.
When firmware determines that Cell-5 charging is appropriate, it may
reconfigure Board 2 ALERT for the charger-control function.

The SK3624 remains responsible for the actual LiFePO4 CC/CV charging
and normal charge termination. SNAPACK controls whether the charging
subsystem is authorized to operate.


IMPLEMENTATION NOTE

Before finalizing this scheme, bench-test that Board 2 ALERT can be
reliably commanded into both required states through ADS1115 register
configuration while retaining the required multi-channel scanning
behavior.
